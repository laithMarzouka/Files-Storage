Assessment Comparison

Patient: 33-year-old male · Assessment 1: 5 Sep 2025 · Assessment 2: 5 May 2026 (242 days later)

Main takeaway

The two assessments point in different directions, and that split is the most important thing here. Measured attention performance improved clearly and general wellbeing and depression self-reports improved. At the same time, self-reported anxiety rose and the EEG shifted broadly toward slower activity, moving several focus-related ratios out of their normal ranges. That EEG change runs the same direction in nearly every band — the pattern a genuine slowing produces, but also the pattern a difference in alertness or recording conditions produces. It is contradicted by the performance task, so it should not yet be read as a decline in attention.

What this may mean

Possible explanations, not conclusions:

  • The EEG and the CPT disagree. If the elevated theta/beta ratios reflected a real decline in sustained attention, task performance would be expected to worsen; instead it improved markedly. That argues against reading the EEG slowing as an attention problem on its own.
  • Session state is a live alternative. The reference material notes a beta response while counting below −15% may indicate tiredness when reading or problem solving, and the PHQ-9 fatigue item worsened over the same period. Fatigue on the recording day could plausibly account for much of a global slowing.
  • Wellbeing and anxiety diverged coherently. Improving GHQ-12 and PHQ-9 alongside a rising GAD-7 is internally consistent if worry and difficulty relaxing have become the dominant symptom while mood and overall function lifted.
  • No cause can be established. The data do not record what happened between the two dates, so no change can be attributed to any intervention or circumstance.

Overall verdict

A mixed but broadly encouraging picture. Objective attention performance and general wellbeing both improved clearly; anxiety is the one measure that worsened and is now the leading self-reported concern. The EEG shows a substantial global slowing that is unexplained, is contradicted by the performance data, and carries enough session-state and montage caveats that it should be treated as provisional.

Suggested next steps

  1. Discuss the anxiety rise directly — specifically worry and difficulty relaxing, which recur across GAD-7, ASRS and the PHQ-9 fatigue item.
  2. Clarify Assessment 2's recording conditions: time of day, prior night's sleep, caffeine, and alertness or drowsiness during the session.
  3. Review raw signal quality for Assessment 2, especially Tp9, before its values inform interpretation.
  4. Consider repeating the EEG under matched conditions to establish whether the slowing is stable or a one-session state effect.
  5. Explore the worsened fatigue and concentration items, and ask about sleep quality despite PHQ-9 recording no sleep problem, since several EEG checks list sleep disturbance among their possible modifiers.
