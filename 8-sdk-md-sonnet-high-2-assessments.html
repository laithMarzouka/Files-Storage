Overall Verdict and Next Steps

Taken together, this is a mixed picture rather than a uniform improvement. Mood and general-health self-reports improved, and objective attention-task performance (CPT) got measurably better. At the same time, anxiety self-report rose, and several EEG markers tied to theta/beta balance and peak alpha frequency moved further outside the normal range — a pattern the reference material associates with inattention or fatigue, which sits in tension with the improved CPT scores.

Suggested next steps (for clarification and discussion, not treatment changes):

  • Discuss the rise in GAD-7 score with the patient, particularly the "worry" and "trouble relaxing" items, to understand what may be driving it.
  • Review the widening gap between the EEG theta/beta findings (worsening) and the CPT results (improving) with the clinician — these two objective measures point in different directions and deserving of a joint look.
  • Note that Assessment 2 added two EEG channels (Fp2, Tp9) not present in Assessment 1; treat findings unique to those sites as first-time data rather than a trend.
  • Consider a follow-up EEG check focused on peak alpha and the theta/beta ratios, since these showed the clearest and most consistent movement away from normal.
